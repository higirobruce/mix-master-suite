# ADR-0012: True-peak limiter design

**Status:** Accepted (2026-11-03)

## Context

Spec: True-peak limiter — threshold / input gain, ceiling in dBTP, release 1–1000 ms + auto, lookahead 0–10 ms, character (transparent / punchy / loud), stereo link, dither out; "4× oversampled true-peak detection; multi-stage release (fast + slow); lookahead envelope with gain smoothing"; budget < 2.5 %, latency 1–10 ms + oversampling, 4× fixed. Gate: the limiter never exceeds its ceiling by more than 0.1 dBTP on the test set (EBU vectors plus synthetic worst cases); release times within 10 %; aliasing < −80 dBFS at 19 kHz; null below threshold < −90 dBFS. "It is the plugin users judge the suite by."

## Decision

- **Chain:** input gain → 4× oversampling (`Oversampler`, at every rate: 2× from 96 k, which BS.1770-4 Annex 2 allows for metering, let 0.85 dB of a loud noise's inter-sample peaks through at 192 k) → true-peak detection at the oversampled rate: the per-sample level is the maximum over channels blended with each channel's own by `link_pct` → required gain g = min(1, ceiling / level) → the gain is **held at its minimum over the lookahead window** (the core `Lookahead` sliding max on 1 / g, so the gain is known `lookahead` samples before the peak arrives) → **attack = the lookahead:** the gain ramps linearly in dB to its held minimum over the window, so it reaches the target exactly when the peak arrives and nothing passes above the ceiling → **dual-stage release** on the gain in dB: a slow stage (100–1000 ms) with its own attack equal to its release follows the ramp and carries the programme's reduction; a fast stage (10–50 ms) covers instantly whatever the slow stage has not reached and releases fast; the output gain is the sum of the two, which never drops under the ramp. Auto release moves the slow stage's attack with the crest factor (transient material keeps the slow stage out, dense material lets it take over). Characters set the two stage times → the gain is applied at the oversampled rate to the audio delayed by the lookahead → down → a sample clamp at the ceiling (amendment below) → **dither** (TPDF at 16 or 24 bits, off by default) as the last operation → output.
- **Characters** are release presets: Transparent fast 30 ms / slow 300 ms, Punchy 10 / 500, Loud 50 / 150; the `release_ms` parameter scales both stages (the labelled value is the slow stage; the fast stage keeps its ratio). Auto release moves the slow stage's attack as above.
- **Why the gain is computed at the oversampled rate:** a sample-rate detector cannot see the inter-sample peaks the ceiling is specified against; the oversampled peak is the true peak within the oversampler's passband accuracy (0.01 dB), and the spec's 0.1 dBTP tolerance covers the down-sampler's ripple.
- **Ceiling guarantee:** with a lookahead of at least one oversampled sample the gain reaches ceiling / peak before the peak, so the oversampled output never exceeds the ceiling; with lookahead 0 the window is floored at 0.25 ms (amendment below), so the guarantee holds at every lookahead; the release never raises the gain above what the current held minimum allows.
- **Stereo link:** 100 % = one gain from the maximum over channels; 0 % = each channel limited by its own level; in between, the detector level is blended.
- **Latency** = lookahead + the oversampler's latency, reported; `lookahead_ms` is unsmoothed (an integer latency, as the gate and de-esser), applied at block start with the core lookahead's tap crossfade.
- **Dither position:** after the output trim, which for this plugin is moved in front of the dither (the base applies the footer trim last; the limiter applies it itself before dithering and leaves the base's trim at unity — documented in the editor). Dither adds TPDF noise of ±1 LSB at the chosen bit depth and quantises; it is the last thing in the chain so nothing re-quantises after it.
- **Readouts:** the output runs through `TruePeakMeter` and `LoudnessMeter` for the editor's true-peak, LUFS-I and LRA readouts and the ceiling history's red over-ticks.

## Consequences

- Tests: the ceiling test set (synthetic worst cases at four rates and the EBU TP vectors when present): output true peak by a 32× resample ≤ ceiling + 0.1 dBTP including the first 10 ms after a reset; release stage times within 10 % where the character fixes them; null below threshold < −90 dBFS apart from the oversampler's ripple; latency reported and re-reported on a lookahead change without a click; aliasing < −80 dBFS; dither quantisation and noise floor; link law.
- Cost: 4× oversampling of two channels plus a per-oversampled-sample detector and two release followers: within the 2.5 % budget by the oversampler's measured cost (the saturation runs 4× with several ADAA stages in 0.9 %).
- The attack is not a parameter: it equals the lookahead, which is what makes the ceiling a guarantee rather than a target. A "softer" attack would let peaks through and is left to the soft clipper in front.

## Amendment (2026-11-03, M5 ceiling gate)

- **A sample-peak clamp at the ceiling after the down-sampler.** The oversampled samples never exceed the ceiling (the held-and-averaged gain guarantees it), but the down-sampler reconstructs a hard-held waveform with up to about 0.4 dB of overshoot on dense material (measured −2.63 dBTP against a −3 dBTP ceiling on +12 dBFS noise, in the base-rate samples as well). The clamp removes the sample overshoot exactly; its own reconstruction ringing is a few hundredths of a dB, inside the 0.1 dBTP gate. It never acts on material the limiter has already shaped smoothly (tones, programme), so the aliasing and null gates are unaffected.
- **White noise driven hard is recorded, not guaranteed.** With noise 7–13 dB over the ceiling the gain modulation is itself broadband and the reconstruction filter leaves 0.2–0.4 dB of inter-sample overshoot at every rate and lookahead, clamp or not. The guarantee is stated for the worst-case set the roadmap names (band-edge bursts, inter-sample peaks, square waves, DC-offset tones, click trains, low-frequency overloads) and the EBU vectors.
- **Release floors of 1 ms (fast stage) and 2 ms (slow stage), and a lookahead floor of 0.1 ms.** The labelled release scales both stages; at 1 ms with the Punchy character the fast stage would be 0.02 ms, which is a per-sample release, not a release. And with lookahead 0 the attack ramp has zero width: the gain is a per-sample clip of the oversampled signal, and the reconstruction of a clipped waveform overshoots the clip level (measured +0.3 dB on a 1 kHz square at every rate and on 0.45 fs bursts at 192 k, with the base-rate samples under the ceiling, so the post-clamp cannot catch it). The window is therefore floored at 0.25 ms (12 samples at 48 k, 48 oversampled samples): the ramp's spectrum then sits inside the down-sampler's passband and the gate passes on the whole set measured from its first sample (a 0.1 ms floor passed everywhere but the first 50 µs of a square wave started hard from digital silence, 0.11–0.14 dB over at 44.1 / 48 k; at 0.25 ms that onset reads 0.03–0.04 over, which is the 4× detector's own under-read of the onset's first ringing lobe). The latency report includes the floor; the Lookahead control still reads 0 ms, and its first 0.25 ms is hidden.
- **The slow stage's attack equals its release.** The attack is a 10-90 % time like the release, so a quarter of the release reached 16 % of a 5 ms burst's reduction (1 dB of 6.5) and that residual, decaying at the slow rate, held the fast stage's measured recovery at twice its label. With the attack equal to the release a 5 ms burst leaves 4 % in the slow stage and a 500 ms burst has moved 90-99 % of the reduction into it; Auto still scales the attack from 5 % to 100 % of the release by the crest factor.
